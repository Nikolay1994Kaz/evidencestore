# Copy, restore and the witness

[← README](../README.md)

## Copy and restore

With `replica.url` set (`s3://bucket/prefix?endpoint=…&region=…` or `file:///path`), the evidence log and the chain are copied in immutable chunks, together with signed chain heads. Nothing is ever deleted from the copy, and keys never go there. S3 credentials for the copy come from `AWS_ACCESS_KEY_ID` and `AWS_SECRET_ACCESS_KEY` (with `AWS_SESSION_TOKEN` if set); the region is `region=` in the URL, otherwise `AWS_REGION`, otherwise `us-east-1`. The directory of a `file://` URL is not created by the store, since a missing one looks like an unmounted NFS or rclone mount: create it on the mounted disk, as a directory inside it rather than the mount point itself. Until it is there, every pass fails (`evidencestore_replica_errors_total`) and nothing lands on the local disk. `evidencestore restore` rebuilds the log and the chain from the copy and verifies the chain links against the latest signed head whose link the copy holds. The chain is cut at that head: a pass that was interrupted before its head may have left links whose leaves never arrived, or leaves whose links never did, and nothing the head does not sign is taken; records past the head are linked again from the evidence log when the store starts. The restored store does not write on the copy in place, since its bytes would land on that cut tail: it writes the copy again as a new generation, from scratch, under `gen/<n>/` in the same location. Earlier generations stay as they are, and `restore` takes the newest generation that holds a signed head. On its first pass after start the store also checks that the copy ends where `data_dir` does. If `data_dir` lost data that had already been copied (a disk that lied about fsync), or did not write that copy, the store refuses to write on it (`the copy is not where data_dir left it` in the log, `evidencestore_replica_errors_total` grows): restore from the copy into an empty `data_dir`, or point `replica.url` at a new location. The evidence log and the leaves are not checked against the chain at that point: a damaged chunk shows when the store starts (frame checksum) or when a proof of its records is built, so do not discard another copy of `data_dir` on the strength of `restore` alone. `keyring.dat` and `sign.key` come back from `keys.backup_dir` (the store checks `sign.key` against that copy on every start, and refuses to start with a chain but without `sign.key`), the roots from `keys.roots_mirror_dir`. On start the store checks that every record in the log is sealed with a key that `keyring.dat` knows: a wrong or too old `keyring.dat` stops the start instead of leaving bodies unreadable. What the store accepted after the last copy is lost with the disk: collectors keep the signed promises as proof, and the next record or heartbeat of each source shows the gap in the chain. So is a collector binding made after the last copy: that source is bound again to the first installation that contacts the restored store, and a legitimate one refused meanwhile is accepted with `sources bind`. A record whose evidence reached the copy but whose chain link did not is linked when the restored store starts, in links no longer than a chain tick by receive time, as the lost ones were, each carrying the receive time of the last record it links: the promise check reads time-stamps, not link times, and no time-stamp is taken before they are linked, so it does not show that delay; for a Pipelock source, the entry after a lost one is marked as not intact (its chain link to the previous entry is broken).

## Witness of the chain heads

Whoever holds `sign.key` can sign a second version of the chain, and a bundle from it verifies on its own. Only someone who saw a head early and keeps it out of that administrator's reach can show that the chain was rewritten: a witness. Each link carries the hash of the one before, so a head the witness holds fixes the whole chain up to it.

With `witness.url` set (`s3://bucket/prefix?endpoint=…&region=…` or `file:///path`), the store sends the witness its signed chain heads only — number, hash, link count, signing time, key and signature; no records and no metadata — under `heads/<number>.json` (the number zero-padded to 20 digits): the head under every TSA time-stamp, where a single-record bundle ends, the head of the link that holds its token, where window and one-source bundles end, and the current head every `witness.every` (1 minute by default). With a copy configured, the witness never gets ahead of it: only heads the copy already holds after a successful pass are sent, so a restore from the copy cannot contradict the witness, and heads reach it up to `replica.every` later. S3 credentials come only from `ES_WITNESS_ACCESS_KEY_ID` and `ES_WITNESS_SECRET_ACCESS_KEY` (and `ES_WITNESS_SESSION_TOKEN`), never from the copy's; the region is found as for the copy. `witness.url` must differ from `replica.url`. As with the copy, the directory of a `file://` witness is not created: while it is missing, every send fails (`evidencestore_witness_errors_total`).

The witness has to be run by someone the store's administrator does not control: the auditor, another organization, or a cloud bucket with S3 Object Lock in compliance mode, which not even the account owner can delete from before the retention ends. S3 on a host where that administrator has root is a backup, not a witness: root deletes or rewrites its files past S3. Give the store a user that may only write objects, and keep versioning on: an overwrite then adds a version, and the original one stays. Tried on MinIO with Object Lock: a locked version cannot be deleted, but a newer version or a delete marker hides it from a plain read and from the listing, so take all versions.

An auditor checks a bundle against the witness with `esverify -heads DIR`, where `DIR` holds the heads they took from the witness themselves, all versions (for S3, `aws s3api list-object-versions` with `get-object --version-id`, or `rclone copy --s3-versions`; any S3-compatible storage, through `--endpoint-url`). Files that are not heads signed with the store key are skipped. Of the heads at or after the record's link, the one the witness received first fixes the record: a chain rewritten after the witness saw that head does not agree with it. `esverify` takes the first such head by number instead and does not yet know when the witness received a head: see the next paragraph. Two different heads with one number prove that the store signed two versions of the chain. Where that touches the record's part of the chain, the bundle does not verify; elsewhere the record stays proven, and the report names the second version: a rewrite, or a store that lost data without a copy. If the head `esverify` takes lies past the end of the bundle, ask for a longer one: `evidencestore proof … -to-head N`. Anything else the administrator cannot take back works as a witness too, for example `evidencestore statement` mailed or published once a day: check against those heads the same way, in a `DIR` of their own.

That leaves a gap: a head signed later and added to the witness under an unused number between the record's link and the head that fixes it comes first by number, and `esverify` takes it instead. Whoever holds both `sign.key` and the witness credentials can add one (P-21 in [`docs/dev/FINDINGS.md`](dev/FINDINGS.md)). The store sends its heads in the order of their numbers, so a head added that way reached the witness after heads with higher numbers, and that gives it away. Until `esverify` checks this, check it yourself, for each witness on its own with a `DIR` of its own; the bundle has to pass against each. `esverify` names the head it took (`head N`, in its line about the witness's heads): no head with a higher number may have reached that witness before the earliest version of `heads/<N>.json` did. If one did, head N was added later and fixes nothing: ask for a bundle up to the first head above N that passes this check (`evidencestore proof … -to-head M`) and verify it again. `esverify` names head N again, but the bundle now reaches M and has to agree with it. S3 gives each version the time it received it (`LastModified` in `list-object-versions`): on AWS the writer cannot choose it, and on other S3-compatible storage check that a user who may only write objects cannot set it. For a statement mailed to you, take the time your mail server received it, not the date in the message; for a published one, its date. A `file://` witness keeps no such time, since whoever writes a file sets its time: it works only with snapshots you take of it on a schedule, a head then counts as received at your first snapshot that holds it, and a rewrite made before that snapshot is not caught.
