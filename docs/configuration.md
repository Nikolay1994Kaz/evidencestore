## Store

See [`configs/evidencestore.example.json`](../configs/evidencestore.example.json) and [`configs/collector.example.json`](../configs/collector.example.json). The example time-stamps with the public DigiCert TSA (`configs/tsa/digicert`); any RFC 3161 service works if its certificates are listed in `tsa.trust`, e.g. `http://tsp.pki.gov.kz` with `configs/tsa/nca-kz/*.cer` in Kazakhstan. Tokens are verified before they enter the chain. With a TSA configured, receive times are kept between the time-stamps that `esverify` checks them against: the store knows TSA time within a window from its last time-stamp and the monotonic clock, and a receive time is the host clock held inside that window and never moving ahead faster than the monotonic clock (a host clock that steps forward is followed gradually), so neither a host clock running ahead nor one lagging behind or stepping makes a record unverifiable. The intake accepts records only while that window is narrow enough and the process has taken two time-stamps: the first one only sets the window (whatever entered the chain while it was under way carries the raw host clock), and the store takes the second right after it. Before that, and when the last time-stamp is about three days old (about 75 hours; less if the TSA answered slowly), it answers `503` and collectors keep the lines in their spool. The store requests a time-stamp at start, retries every 10 seconds while the intake waits (less often after failures in a row, down to every 2 minutes), and stamps at least once an hour even when nothing new entered the chain; a TSA that stays unreachable for about three days closes the intake. Every source is registered by name. The name must equal the CommonName of the collector's client certificate, and the certificate can be pinned by its SHA-256 fingerprint. The source is always taken from the certificate, never from the request.

Risky values are rejected at start instead of being silently replaced: negative periods (zero or absent means the default), `keys.min_wait` under one hour, `chain.tick` under 10 milliseconds or above 2.5 seconds, `chain.promise_mmd` under 55 seconds plus two ticks or above a minute, `roots_dir` and `data_dir` nested in either direction, a `content_type` over 255 bytes, an empty `listen`, a `witness.cosigners` entry whose `url` is not `http://` or `https://` with a host and without a query or whose `key` is not a verifier key, a witness listed twice, more than 8 of them, an `old_keys` entry that is not a verifier key or repeats a key already listed, more than 4 old keys for a witness, a `tsa.archive.url` that is not `http://` or `https://` with a host, names the same service as `tsa.url` (the same host, port, path and query, whatever the case of the host) or comes without `tsa.url` or without `tsa.archive.trust`. A demo stand that must destroy a key within minutes sets `"demo": true`; never in production, where it leaves seconds to cancel a destruction. Without a `tsa` section the store starts with a warning: its proofs then carry no time-stamp, and `esverify` rejects them unless told `-allow-no-tsa`; bundles over a window of time cannot be built at all.

Chain heads can go to a witness bucket (`witness.url`) and to witnesses that cosign them (`witness.cosigners`, C2SP): see [Copy, restore and the witness](copy-and-witness.md).

A second TSA of another operator, `"tsa": {"archive": {"url": "…", "trust": ["…"]}}`, time-stamps the chain head on its own every `tsa.every`; its tokens go into the bundles and show that the time-stamps of the first TSA existed earlier, should its certificate be revoked for key compromise (see [Revoked TSA certificates](bundles.md#revoked-tsa-certificates)). It does not take turns with the first: receive times follow the clock of the first TSA, and checking them against another TSA's clock could fail an honest record over a fraction of a second. Choose a TSA whose CA publishes CRLs: its time-stamp is a proof only when the auditor can check its own certificate (give the auditor the CRLs of its CA), and keep those CRLs while its certificate is valid. Its tokens are verified with `tsa.archive.trust` before they are kept, and when a token does not carry the TSA's certificate, the journal keeps the certificate from `tsa.archive.trust` with it; once kept, they go into the bundles even after `tsa.archive` points at another service or is removed, so changing the second TSA does not take back what the earlier one vouched for: a bundle carries the earliest token of each signing key of them (the auditor trusts the one they choose). A second TSA that signs with the key of `tsa.url` is the same TSA under another address: its tokens are not kept, and the error shows in `evidencestore status` and the metrics. `tsa.archive.url` with the same host, port, path and query as `tsa.url` is refused at start.

One process per `data_dir`: `serve`, `init` and `restore` take a lock on `data_dir/LOCK` before writing anything. The store also keeps `data_dir/tree.hashes`, the hashes of the Merkle tree over the chain links (about 64 bytes per link) from which bundles and the collectors' proofs are built. It is derived from the chain: every start checks each of its hashes against the chain (with a link every second, a year of chain makes the file about 2 GB, read once per start, next to the scan of every leaf the start does anyway) and rewrites it from the first one that does not match, and the store signs a root taken from it only after checking that root against the tree it keeps in memory. If writing it fails while the store runs, bundles are refused until a restart (the log says so).

A source whose lines are about many people can name the field that says whose a line is: `"subject_field": "client"` (a top-level field of a JSON line, at most 64 bytes; see [One subject](bundles.md)). The store labels each record at intake, and `evidencestore proof -subject` then opens one subject's records and no others. Records taken in before the field was set carry no label, and they stay that way. A record keeps the field it was labelled by: after a change, `-subject` follows the new field, and records under the old one stay closed, so pick the field once. A retired source keeps its field, with `"disabled": true`: without it the store cannot name the labels of the source's records. It is not for `pipelock/recorder`: the store's verdicts show the session of every record in the open. The labels come from the month keys in `keyring.dat`: each month of the source derives a label key of its own, so a client's labels live and die with the month (see [Keys](keys.md)). A `data_dir` written by the development build of 7 November 2026 (commit `edae072`, labels from `sign.key`, record version 2) does not carry over: once it holds a record with such a label, the store refuses to start on it and names the reason; keep it for reference and start a new one. Its window and one-source bundles (version 4) are then not issued again in this release's format either. A source without `subject_field` writes its records as before. Once a record with a label is written, v0.4.0 no longer starts on that `data_dir`.

## Collector

`escollect` needs the store's public key pinned as `store_key` (printed by `evidencestore init`) and an `https://` server address; risky values (`http://`, `batch` above 512, a negative `flush_every` or `heartbeat_every`, a `checkpoint_every` under a second) are rejected at load instead of being silently replaced. The collector keeps the store's chain heads as a witness (`checkpoint_every`, 10 minutes by default: see [Collectors as witnesses](copy-and-witness.md#collectors-as-witnesses)). Lines wait in an on-disk spool (`spool_dir`), each with its file position, so a crash never renumbers them. A record counts as delivered only when the store answers with a signed promise that verifies against the pinned key and names that very record; otherwise the record stays in the spool and the bad promise is kept in `promises-rejected.jsonl` as evidence against the store. The collector also checks the receive time in each promise against its own clock: the store's receive time never runs ahead of real time, so one more than a minute later than the store's answer means the store shifts receive times forward, which could move a record out of the window it arrived in. The collector logs an error and keeps such a promise in `promises-ahead.jsonl`; the record counts as delivered, since the store has it. It checks the deadline too: one more than a minute after the receive time would let a record held back past the time-stamp that closes its window still meet it, so the collector logs an error and keeps such a promise in `promises-deadline.jsonl`, and the record counts as delivered (`esverify -promise` holds a record to a minute after its receive time anyway). Keep the collectors' clocks within a minute of real time (NTP).

The collector stops instead of handing out numbers the store already holds: when `spool_dir` was restored from an old copy (a VM snapshot, a backup), or when a copy of the same installation runs next to it. The store, in turn, compares the body of every resent number with the accepted one and refuses a batch carrying another body under a taken number (`409`, `"code": "seq_conflict"`, a conflict event in the chain). It also refuses a resend it cannot compare, because the accepted body sits under a month key pending destruction (cancelling the destruction lets the resend through) or already destroyed; the store names the reason, and the collector logs it as is.

Every collector installation has an epoch: a random id kept in `spool_dir/epoch.json` and sent with every request. The store binds a source to the first installation it hears from (an event in the chain) and refuses any other installation that uses the source certificate, already at start, before it reads a line (`409`, `"code": "epoch_mismatch"`). A legitimate reinstall (`spool_dir` lost or emptied) is accepted by the operator with `evidencestore sources bind -source S -epoch E`; the refusal names the command, and the previous installation is refused from then on. Copies of one installation (a VM snapshot, a copied `spool_dir`) share the epoch; the number checks above catch them.

With nothing to send, the collector sends a heartbeat every `heartbeat_every` (30 seconds by default): `GET /v1/state`, the request it makes at start, with the last sequence number it issued. A gap the collector left at the end of its numbering (a file that vanished before it was read through) reaches the chain with that heartbeat, not only with the next record of the source. The heartbeat comes from the loop that reads the log, so a hung collector goes quiet like a killed one, and a refusal of its epoch stops it even when it has nothing to send: after `sources bind` the previous installation stops within a heartbeat. The store remembers the last request of each source it let through (a refused installation does not count). When a source's collector has not been heard from for longer than the source's `expect_contact` (2 minutes by default, at least 1 minute), the store writes "collector lost" into the chain with the last contact and raises `evidencestore_source_contact_lost`; when the collector is back, it writes the window without contact. `status` shows `last_contact` and `contact_lost` per source. Time counts from the store's own start, so a store that was down does not report its collectors lost, and an open episode survives a restart. A quiet log with a live collector is not a lost collector but silence (`expect_every`). Lines written while the collector was away are read when it is back, with receive times after the window; lines deleted meanwhile are not, and the window shows when that could happen.

If the spool (`spool_dir/spool/`) is lost while `state.json` or `files.json` in `spool_dir` survived, the collector refuses to start: restore `spool_dir` from a backup. Emptying it starts over as a new installation (bind it as above) from the store's last number and skips lines that were not delivered; with `from_start` the whole log is delivered again under new numbers, so the store holds duplicates. Emptying `spool_dir` also drops the collector's head journal and conflict file (see [Collectors as witnesses](copy-and-witness.md#collectors-as-witnesses)): move them aside first if you keep them as evidence.

This release does not read what v0.3.1 or earlier wrote: the Merkle trees of the chain now carry a flag for every time-stamp leaf (see [Bundles](bundles.md)), and a chain head is a checkpoint over a tree of the links rather than the hash of the last link; the store refuses an older `data_dir` and says why. Set it up as a new installation: a new `data_dir` and key directories, and a new location for the copy and the witness, since the new chain starts again from link 1 and its heads would read there as a second version of the old one. Collectors start over too, since the new store has a new key and knows none of their numbers: let each deliver what it holds to the old store, then give it an empty `spool_dir` and the new store's public key as `store_key`. Without `from_start` it reads from the end of the log; with it, the whole log goes to the new store again. Upgrade the store before the collectors: collectors of this version log an error for every promise whose deadline is more than a minute after its receive time and keep it in `promises-deadline.jsonl`, and an older store with `chain.promise_mmd` above a minute gives such promises. An older collector sends no epoch; the store accepts it until the source is bound to an installation. Collectors of v0.2.0 and earlier send no heartbeat either: with this store their quiet minutes read as a lost collector, so upgrade them soon after the store. A configuration with `chain.promise_mmd` under 55 seconds plus two chain ticks (the demo scripts had 10 seconds) or above a minute is now refused at start: with a shorter deadline an honest store could miss its own promise. So is a `chain.tick` above 2.5 seconds, which leaves no room for such a deadline. `esverify -promise` of this version holds a record to a minute after its receive time whatever deadline the promise names: a promise from an older store with `chain.promise_mmd` above a minute verifies only for records before which no time-stamp taken more than a minute after their receive time lies in the chain. `esverify` of this version checks the time of every link that holds a time-stamp token it is shown: a chain written by v0.2.0 or earlier on a host whose clock ran more than a minute behind the TSA when it took a time-stamp fails that check in the bundles that show that token. There is no way back: once this release has put a record into the chain, v0.2.0 does not start on that `data_dir`, nor on one restored from a copy this release wrote.

The collector keeps a registry of the files it reads, by inode, each with its own read position, first line, birth time and quiet timer; it is written to the spool before a file's first line and survives a crash. Every file at the log path (or under the mask) is opened as soon as the collector sees it and read while it grows. A file that leaves the path — rotated, moved out of the mask (`*.log` and `app.log` → `app.log.1`), deleted — is read through its open descriptor until it has been quiet for 10 seconds after it left (applications keep writing into a rotated file until they reopen their log), and only then the collector takes a last line without a newline and closes it. Files are read independently, so a second rotation during that wait, or two applications writing under one mask, lose nothing; lines of different files interleave in the numbering, lines of one file keep their order. After a restart a rotated file is found by its inode in the same directory, under any name, and recognised by its birth time. A file truncated or replaced in place, or a known file that is gone after a restart, costs one sequence number: the store records the gap in the chain, so the loss is visible. Reading goes on at the `flush_every` pace while the store is unreachable; only sending backs off. A file the collector cannot open (permissions after logrotate `create`) is still registered: it is read as soon as it can be opened, wherever it has moved in its directory, and if it disappears first, its loss is a gap. A directory that cannot be listed (permissions, out of file descriptors, a file-system error) is not taken for an empty one: nothing is concluded from that pass, and files already open are read on. A missing log file, an empty glob, a file that cannot be opened or a directory that cannot be listed is logged once a minute.

With `glob`, wildcards are allowed in the file name only: the collector lists that one directory and looks for rotated files in it. The registry remembers how far every file under the mask has been read (`files.json`), so a shard whose modification time lands in the past (clock stepped back) is still read; a finished file under the mask is closed after a minute of quiet when a newer one exists, and opened again if it grows. Limits: after a restart, a compressed or moved-away rotation (`compress` without `delaycompress`, `olddir`) is not found, and its unread tail becomes a sequence gap. The collector connects to the store directly and ignores `HTTPS_PROXY`.
