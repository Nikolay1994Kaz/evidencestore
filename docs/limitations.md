# Known limitations

[← README](../README.md)

- **A file the collector never saw.** A file that appears at the log path (or under the mask) and leaves it while the collector is not looking — it was stopped, or both happened within one `flush_every` — is never read, and no gap is recorded. Likewise, lines that are written and destroyed (deleted, truncated) between two reads leave no trace — and so do lines of a file the collector cannot open yet, if the file is truncated in place before it can be read. Every file the collector has seen is followed until it is read or its loss is recorded as a gap.
- **A file closed after a minute of quiet** (`glob` only, when a newer file exists) that is then written again and destroyed within one `flush_every` loses those lines without a gap.
- **Long lines.** A line is read into the collector's memory whole before it is cut, so a source writing one line of hundreds of megabytes inflates the collector. A line longer than 1 047 552 bytes becomes several records with no continuation mark; the cut may fall inside a UTF-8 character, and for `pipelock/recorder` every piece fails the gateway-entry check.
- **`copytruncate` rotation** costs one sequence number whether or not unread lines were lost: the collector cannot tell the two cases apart.
- **The collector epoch is an installation id, not a secret.** Whoever copies the source certificate together with `spool_dir` also has `epoch.json`. Protect `spool_dir` as you protect the certificate key.
- **Gateway journals.** The store checks the gateway's signed checkpoint. Signatures of individual receipts inside Pipelock entries, their `chain_prev_hash` chains and the `chain-link` file between sessions are not verified.
- **A gap event can be missing after a crash.** When the store crashes right after a record that skips sequence numbers, power fails while the chain writes its leaves, or a restore from the copy cuts the chain at its signed head, the record may already be in the chain while the explicit gap event is not, and the event is not announced after restart. The gap itself stays visible in the chain from the record numbers.
- **Key backup unreachable at restart.** `keyring.dat` knows which of its keys have reached `keys.backup_dir` only up to its previous write, so after a restart the keys created by its last write are not used until the copy succeeds again. If `keys.backup_dir` is unreachable at that moment (a network share is down), intake for those months waits: right after installation until the next keyring change, and in the first minutes of a month. Nothing is lost — collectors keep the lines — and `evidencestore_keyring_backup_pending` shows it.
- **Approvals are recorded, not enforced.** A destruction request needs two distinct approver names (`keys schedule -approve A -approve B`), and the chain keeps them with the request, but the store does not authenticate them: whoever reaches the admin socket (the store's user, root on its host) can give both, and names that differ only in case count as two. The waiting period from a TSA time-stamp applies all the same. Dual control that holds against the host's administrator needs a check that administrator cannot reach, such as an HSM quorum; it is not built.
- **Bulk proof export.** A single-record bundle is built under the chain lock. It reads the whole leaf file (`leaves_*.log`, up to 64 MiB before it rotates) that holds the record's leaf, the T1 or the T2 token, three or four times per bundle (once more for the leaves from the head that T1 certifies to the record's link), and `chain.log` from the start up to the bundle's last link; while the TSA is unreachable, the leaves since the last time-stamp grow with everything taken in, and so do the bundle and the time it takes. At about 77 thousand records (one 6 MB leaf file) that took 50–100 ms per bundle on a loaded host (measured before version 3 bundles added a read), almost all of it reading the leaf file, and intake waits meanwhile. Export many single bundles off-peak, or ask for a bundle over a window of time: it is built without holding the chain lock and reads every leaf file once, up to 250 000 leaves per bundle. A one-source bundle holds the lock only to find the source's last record before the window, a pass over that source's records: 6 ms at 300 thousand records, so about 0.2 s at 10 million.
- **A stalled `keys.backup_dir` stalls the intake and the time-stamps.** A new month's key is not used until its copy has landed in `keys.backup_dir`, and while a key is being created and copied (from the last week of a month the store prepares the next month's), records of every source wait for it. A copy that hangs (a hard-mounted NFS that stopped answering) holds every record that arrives, and a time-stamp waits for the records taken in before it, so none is taken either and windows do not close until the copy finishes. The store logs the records in flight (`time-stamp deferred`); nothing is lost, the collectors keep the lines.
- **A receive time shifted by up to two minutes goes unnoticed.** The receive time is the store's word, and the collector catches only a shift of more than a minute past its answer by its own clock (`promises-ahead.jsonl`), which may itself run up to a minute ahead: a store can move a record that arrived in the last two minutes of a window into the next one.
- **A held-back record shows only with the collector's promise.** A store owner who holds a record back past the time-stamp that closes its window keeps it out of the window bundle, and the record's own bundle verifies. The missed deadline shows when someone checks the collector's promise for that record (`esverify -promise`: the token of the closing time-stamp lies before the record); nothing compares the promises by itself.
- **The heartbeat shows a running collector, not an honest one.** Root on the source host holds the collector's certificate, key and epoch, so it can keep the heartbeat going itself while the collector is stopped. A stop shorter than `expect_contact` leaves no event either: a collector stopped for a minute and started again is not reported.
- **A bundle names its own source.** `esverify` has no way to be told which source you meant. A bundle for a name the source tails do not list verifies and holds no records, so a real bundle with the name changed to a similar one and its records removed passes. The report prints the name in quotes and says that the tails do not name it, instead of "no records": compare it with the source you expect.
- **A changed record shows at the next start, and it stops the store.** Every start checks each record already in the chain against the digest in its leaf, and refuses to start on one that differs: restore `data_dir` from a copy taken before the change, and keep the changed one for the investigation. If the copy already holds the change, or there is no copy, the store cannot start: nothing in the product gets past such a record, and one record keeps every other one out of reach. A restore also loses what was accepted after the last pass of the copy. Records past the last signed head have no leaf yet, are not checked, and are linked as they lie. A running store does not check the log, so a record changed under it shows at the next start, or when a bundle of that record is checked. The check makes a start about twice as long: on a test machine under load, a month of 800 000 records took 6 seconds instead of 3, and memory at start went from 53 to 120 MB.
- **Index in memory.** The sequence-to-position and position-to-leaf indexes are rebuilt from the evidence log at start and take 16 bytes per record in memory; the index of chain links takes 24 bytes per link, and with a link every second that is about 0.75 GB a year.
- **Without receipt times the witness check goes by number.** With the S3 versions list or a collector's head journal in the heads directory, `esverify -heads` takes, for each witness, the head it received first. Without them — a mailed statement, a `file://` witness, a directory without the list — it takes the first head at or after the record's link by number, and the report says so: whoever holds both `sign.key` and the witness credentials can then sign a head later and add it under an unused number between the record's link and the head that fixes it, and `esverify` takes that head instead (P-21 in [`docs/dev/FINDINGS.md`](dev/FINDINGS.md)). Check the head it names yourself, as Witness of the chain heads describes. And no witness catches a rewrite made before the first head after the record reached it (`witness.every`, a minute by default).
- **Witness queue in memory.** Heads waiting for an unreachable witness are kept in memory: every head under a time-stamp, the head of its token's link and the latest current one. Those not sent before the store stops are not resent after the restart; for their records, ask for a bundle up to the next head the witness holds (`proof -to-head`). The heads cannot be read back through `evidencestore`: take them from the witness with an S3 tool that lists versions. The same goes for each cosigning witness: heads it did not cosign before the store stopped are not offered again, and the records under them are fixed by the next head it cosigns, later.
- **Cosignatures after the last copy pass.** The cosignatures of the C2SP witnesses go to the copy with the evidence log and come back with `restore`, but those received after the last pass are lost with `data_dir`, and a witness keeps only its latest head: a record whose head was cosigned in that time is fixed, after the restore, by the next head the witness cosigns (ask for a bundle up to it, `proof -to-head`). The journal and its index in memory grow with every cosigned head, about 1 700 a day for each witness of a busy store (a head every `witness.every` and two under every time-stamp), and the start reads the journal through.
- **Ed25519 cosignatures only.** C2SP recommends ML-DSA-44 cosignatures, which a quantum computer cannot forge, for new witnesses; this release makes and checks Ed25519 cosignatures (`cosignature/v1`) only, and a witness key of another type is refused in the configuration.
- **A witness can be pushed ahead of the copy.** A witness bucket takes heads only from the store; a cosigning witness takes them from anyone who holds a head signed by the store and can make a consistency proof for it, such as the end of a bundle. Such a head can lie past the copy, and after a restore from the copy the witness refuses the restored chain from there (`422`), as without a copy.
- **A cosigning witness is trusted for its clock and key.** The time in a cosignature is the witness's clock, and whoever controls the witness's host or key can cosign any history of the store, at any time. A witness that lost its state refuses to start while its journal of cosigned heads survives; one whose whole `data_dir` is gone can go on only under a new key, and nothing but its operator's word tells auditors so. The witness also learns every head it cosigns: how fast the store grows, not what it holds.
- **The revocation of TSA certificates is checked only against what the auditor brings.** `esverify` is offline: it reads full CRLs of the issuing CAs and the auditor's own lists (`-tsa-crl`, `-tsa-revoked`), not OCSP; delta and indirect CRLs are refused, and a CRL signed with a new key of the CA (after it changed its key) is not used: the certificates it would cover count as not checked. A CRL names a certificate, not a key: a key under suspicion in one time-stamp of a bundle taints every time-stamp of the bundle signed with it, whatever certificate it carries, but if no time-stamp of the bundle carries a revoked certificate of that key, another certificate the CA issued for it and did not revoke is not seen as compromised unless the auditor lists it too. A time-stamp of a key revoked for compromise counts only with a later time-stamp of another TSA or a witness cosignature before the cutoff ([Revoked TSA certificates](bundles.md#revoked-tsa-certificates)); without a second TSA (`tsa.archive`) or a cosigning witness, every bundle that rests on such a key fails, and with them those resting on the time-stamps made shortly before the cutoff (within `tsa.every` for the second TSA, a witness pass for a witness) still do. Whether a revocation date is honest is the CA's word. The journal of the second TSA keeps a token of a few kilobytes every `tsa.every`, some 100–300 MB a year at the default ten minutes, and the start reads it through.
- **The witness follows the copy.** With `replica.url` set, heads reach the witness only after the copy holds them, so the copy being down stalls the witness too, and after a restore the witness waits until the new copy generation is written. Without a copy a lost disk cannot be restored, and a store that lost the tail of its chain (a disk that lied about fsync) writes other links under numbers the witness saw: a second version of the chain, which the report names.
- **Collectors see the store's pace.** Every answer gives a collector the store's signed head, so it learns how many links the chain has and how fast the whole store grows, all sources together, though not what the store holds. Its head journal (`checkpoints.jsonl`) says the same to whoever reads it.
- **A collector goes on from the head it saved last.** The collector writes its last head to `spool_dir` at most every `checkpoint_every` and when it stops, not on every answer, and after a crash it goes on from the head it saved last: a rewrite of the chain after that head, made before the crash, is no longer caught by that collector.
- **Heads of different lengths are not compared.** `esverify -heads` checks a witness's heads against the chain in the bundle, and two heads of the same number of links against each other. A store that shows two collectors two histories, at heads of different lengths, shows only in a bundle that covers heads of both. Consistency proofs between any two heads, made by the store on request, would close this; they are not built.
- **A head past the copy.** With a copy configured, the witness, the collectors and `statement` get only heads the copy holds, but bundles and TSA time-stamps can cover links the copy does not hold yet. After a restore from the copy those links are written again, differently, so a bundle given out for such a link before the loss no longer agrees with the restored store.
- **Input.** The collector reads files only: no journald, no syslog receiver, no database tables, no Windows Event Log. rsyslog bridges most of these into files the collector reads: it takes the messages journald gets (install it where it is missing, for example `apt install rsyslog` on Debian 12), receives syslog over the network (`imudp`, `imtcp`, or TLS), and most databases can write their audit to a file or to syslog (PostgreSQL with pgaudit, MySQL, Oracle, MS SQL). The bridge runs next to the source, so whoever controls that host can stop it, just like the collector.
- **Untested failure paths.** Recovery from a failed `fsync` of a directory or of a fresh `sign.key`, and from an S3 listing without a marker, is checked by reading the code, not by tests: those paths need a hardware or service fault.
