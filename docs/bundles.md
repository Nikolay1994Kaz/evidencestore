# Bundles over a window of time

[← README](../README.md)

A single-record bundle proves one record and says nothing about the records the auditor was not given. A bundle over a window of time (RFC 3339 times, by the store's receive time) answers "everything received between FROM and TO" and also proves that nothing is left out. It is bounded by two TSA time-stamps: the last one taken more than a second before FROM and the first one taken more than two minutes after TO. A time-stamp certifies the chain up to the moment the store asked for it; whatever entered the chain while the request was under way lies after that point and is certified only by the next time-stamp. The store does not use a TSA answer that took longer than 30 seconds, and a receive time lags real time by at most 55 seconds, so such a record carries a receive time later than two minutes before the time-stamp, outside the window. Nor does a token enter the chain before the records received before its TSA answer, and it lies in a link of its own: while the store waits for those records, new records wait a moment before they are taken in, and if one of them is still being written five seconds later (a stalled disk), the store drops the answer and the next time-stamp is taken on schedule. Receive times follow the last time-stamp and the host's monotonic clock, which stands still while the host is suspended or its VM paused: after such a pause they lag by its length until the next time-stamp. The store asks for one as soon as the host clock jumps ahead of its estimate, and a TSA answer whose time lies beyond the receive-time window is not used: the store pauses the intake until the records already received reach the chain (collectors retry on `503`; a record still being written after five seconds keeps the intake paused until it lands, and then the store moves its estimate to that TSA time without asking again), moves its estimate to the TSA time and stamps again, so whatever arrived during that request lies before the time-stamp and every receive time follows one estimate or the other. There are two kinds:

- `evidencestore proof -source S -from FROM -to TO [-dek]`: every record of one source, for an auditor from outside. Other sources stay closed.
- `evidencestore proof -window -from FROM -to TO [-source S -dek]`: every record of every source, for an investigation inside the organization.

**One source.** Every evidence leaf carries the hash of the previous leaf of its source, so the records of a source form a chain of their own inside the store's chain. Right before each TSA request the store writes the source tails, the hash of each source's latest leaf, alone in a link, and that link is what the time-stamp certifies. The lower end of the bundle is the tails of the time-stamp before the window. The upper end needs the opposite bound, that everything put into the chain after it came after the window, and a time-stamp over an end does not give it: one can be taken over old tails at any time and appended to the chain without rewriting anything. So the bundle carries the token of the time-stamp that closes the window, which lies in the chain before the upper end, and the upper end is the tails of the first time-stamp after that token: whatever follows the token entered the chain after its time. A one-source bundle is therefore ready one time-stamp later than a window bundle. It carries both ends and every record of S between them with its Merkle path; in those paths other sources' leaves are only hashes. `esverify` checks that each record refers to the one before it, from the lower tails up to the upper ones: a record left out breaks the reference of the next one or leaves the chain short of the upper tails. Each end is certified by its own time-stamp, so moving an end means rewriting the chain after it and taking fresh time-stamps. A witness (`-heads`) catches such a rewrite; without one, a rewrite made within about an hour of the window cannot be told from an honest bundle by T2 alone. `esverify` also checks every receive time against the time-stamps of the ends. In the tails each source is named by a hash of its name, and its entry, with the kind of the source, is sealed with a salt only the store can derive; the bundle opens only S. Of the other sources the auditor sees how many there are, could test a guessed name, and sees the pace of the whole store: for every link between the ends, when it was made and how many leaves it holds. Their record numbers and which of them wrote what stay closed. The tails are the store's own statements, written when it took the time-stamps: a store that understated them would have lied at that moment, and a window bundle or the hidden record's own bundle shows the lie. For gateway journal records the bundle also carries the store's verdict from intake: records found broken are named in the report, and their bodies are not opened (a single-record bundle shows what is wrong). The tails name a gateway journal as such, so its bundle does not verify without a verdict for every record, with bodies or without; a source whose kind changed between the ends (its configuration did) does not verify either.

The bundle names its source itself, and `esverify` does not know which source you meant: compare the name in the report, printed in quotes, with the one you expect. A name the tails do not list proves nothing: such a bundle verifies and holds no records, whether the source really has none yet or someone took a real bundle, changed the name to a similar one and removed its records. The report says so instead of "no records", and `-json` gives `"source_named": false`.

**Every source.** The window bundle carries every leaf of the chain between the two time-stamps. `esverify` recomputes the Merkle root of every link from its leaves and checks that the leaves continue right after the lower head: a record left out, added or moved breaks a root. It verifies every time-stamp in the stretch and checks each record's receive time against the time-stamps around its link: not later than the first one over it, not earlier than a minute before the latest time-stamp whose token lies before it, and later than two minutes before any time-stamp whose certified head it lies after. "Latest" is by the time in the time-stamp, not by place: a copy of an old token can be added anywhere later in the chain. Records after the head the upper time-stamp certifies are not in the report and cannot be attached: nothing in the bundle certifies them. Their receive times must still be later than two minutes before that time-stamp, so a store that held its request back to push records of the window behind the certified head is caught. The report lists every source in the window with its record numbers, recorded gaps, silences, a lost collector and seq conflicts, and names number holes that have no recorded gap. It shows the metadata of every source: names, record numbers, receive times, digests. Bodies come only for one source, with `-source S -dek`.

Give your own window with `esverify -from FROM -to TO`; without it the window named in the bundle is used, and the report says so. `esverify -bodies DIR` writes the bodies out byte for byte. With no record received in the window it says so and exits 0, so a script can go window by window; with records and no body to open it names the reason and exits 2. A window bundle does not name the source its keys are for, so it counts as empty only when no source has a record in the window; a one-source bundle knows its source. The window can be closed only by a time-stamp taken more than two minutes after its end: the store takes one every `tsa.every` while records arrive and at least once an hour, and `evidencestore stamp` takes one at once. One bundle spans up to 250 000 leaves of the chain; split longer windows. The window is the store's receive time, not the time written in a line. A record the store held back and put into the chain after the time-stamp that closes the window is not in the window bundle; the collector's promise for it shows the missed deadline (`esverify -promise` on the record's own bundle). That check rests on the time-stamps alone, not on the store's word: `esverify -promise` checks that no time-stamp taken more than a minute after the receive time lies before the record in the chain, whatever deadline the promise names. A store of this release names a deadline from 55 seconds plus two chain ticks to a minute (the collector checks that it is no later than a minute), so the check is at most a few seconds looser than that deadline: a shorter one cannot be checked fairly against TSA time, since a receive time lags it by up to 55 seconds. The time-stamp that closes the window is taken more than two minutes after its end, so a record held back past it fails the check. The single-record bundle carries every leaf from the head that the time-stamp before the record (T1) certifies to the record's link, and `esverify` checks them against the link roots, so no time-stamp in between can be left out. Not from T1's own token: the store picks T1, and a copy of an old token, or a token it held back, can be put anywhere later in the chain, but not before the head it certifies. Link times play no part, so `esverify -promise` passes for an honest store however long a record takes to reach the chain: a time-stamp waits for the records received before its TSA answer, and is dropped if one of them is still being written five seconds later.

**What opens.** The time-stamps rarely fall on the window, so a bundle runs from one to the other and holds more records than you asked about. Completeness needs only their leaves. With `-dek`, only the records received in the window come with their evidence and record keys; the other records between the ends are leaves: number, receive time and digest, no body (in a gateway journal also the store's verdict, which names the session, the entry type and its hash). `esverify` refuses a bundle with evidence that leaves out a record of the window, also when your own `-from` and `-to` are wider than the bundle's window, and `-bodies` writes only the records of your window. `proof -window -source S -dek` keeps the same rules for the records of S.

**One subject.** A source is one system, and one system can write about many people: an AI agent serves thousands of clients through one log, and they all are one source. Set `subject_field` on such a source (see [Configuration](configuration.md)), and at intake the store labels each JSON line with a pseudonym of that field's value and writes the field's name next to it. `evidencestore proof -source S -subject c-104233 -from FROM -to TO` then opens the records of that subject in the window and no others. The other records of the window come as encrypted evidence without keys: their labels can be read, their bodies cannot. Records of the source outside the window are leaves, as above.

It is the same chain of the source, so no record of the source is left out; which of them are the subject's rests on the labels the store set at intake. `esverify` also requires the evidence of every record of the window, checks that the records under the label were labelled by the field the bundle names and that every opened body names the subject in that field, and refuses a record under another label that comes with a key. The report says how many of the window's records carry the label, how many of those are open and how many other records stay closed; `-bodies` writes only the open ones. When no record of the window is under the label, `-bodies` says that and exits 0, as it does for a window with no records, so a script can go window by window; it does not show that the subject has no records (see below). When the label has records in the window and none of them opens, it names the reason and exits 2; when some open, it writes those, and the report counts the rest.

What a subject bundle does not show:

- The store sets the label, when it receives a record, and nobody can compute it afterwards: it cannot change, since it lies under the digest in the chain, but a store dishonest at intake can file a line under another label or none. The chain stays whole, the collector's promise stays valid and the bundle verifies, unlike a line that is simply not delivered, which leaves a gap in the numbers.
- The value is compared as written: `C-1` and `c-1`, or `1` and `1.0`, are two subjects (`104233` and `"104233"` are one). A line that names someone else is not under this label.
- Lines that name no subject carry no label: the report counts those of the window, and `evidencestore_records_without_subject_total` counts them at intake. That is a line without a string or number in that field, with an empty value or one longer than 256 bytes, with the field twice, or that is not a whole JSON object. Records received before the source had `subject_field` carry no label either, whatever their lines say: a subject bundle never opens them.
- The label is an HMAC under a label key of the record's month, derived from that month key, because a client number is short enough to try them all, so an auditor cannot compute a label; the store can. An open body of a month ties that month's label to the subject, and only that month's: a body opened in September says nothing about the October label, and records under a label of a month with no open body are not called the subject's. With no body of the subject open in the window, nothing ties any label, and the report says so: ask for a wider window that opens one of its records, or compare the label with a bundle that does. A wider window is not always possible (see the limit below).
- Of the other records of the window the bundle shows the number, receive time, length and label. A client keeps one label within a month of a source, so the records of one pseudonymous client can be linked within that month, in one bundle and between bundles, but not across months: each month has labels of its own, and a bundle across a month boundary names a label for each month.
- A record under the label that comes without a key has no body to show: the month key was destroyed, or the store held the key back. The report counts them.
- A window takes the same limit of 250 000 leaves, and the evidence of every record of the window travels whole: for all of a client's history, ask window by window.
- Erasure stays per source and month, and it takes the labels with it: the label key of a month comes from its month key, so once that key is destroyed, or while it awaits destruction, no one can compute a label of that month, the store included. The labels stay in the records under their digests, but nothing matches them to a client any more: a subject bundle names no label for such a month, and the report counts its records as records whose owner cannot be told. That the key is gone is the store's word: the claim is not signed, so anyone can take a label out of a bundle, and the report says only that the bundle names no label for those months; the key receipts in the chain show which month keys were destroyed.

A single-record bundle carries every leaf from the head that T1 certifies to the record's link, so that `esverify -promise` sees every time-stamp before the record and the store cannot hide one. Only the time-stamps lie there whole; every other leaf is just its hash, so the bundle names no other source and no other record. What shows is the pace of the whole store, as in any bundle: how many leaves each link holds and when the link was made. In the Merkle tree of a link each leaf enters together with a flag that says whether it is a time-stamp, and a leaf shown by its hash counts as not one: a time-stamp shown that way breaks its link's root, and a time-stamp written into the chain with the flag unset breaks the root wherever it is shown whole, at the edges of a window or as T1, so it closes no window. A shown hash costs about 70 bytes as the store serves the bundle, and the bundle still grows with the records taken in since T1: with one record a second and a time-stamp every 10 minutes the whole bundle is up to about 165 KB, with 100 records a second about 4 MB, and while the TSA is unreachable it grows by everything taken in since the last time-stamp.

Every bundle ends in a head signed when the bundle is served: a checkpoint, the number of links and the root of a Merkle tree (RFC 6962) over all of them. The bundle carries the links it needs and, before the first of them, the frontier of that tree, at most one hash per bit of the link number, so `esverify` computes the root at every link of the bundle: the signed head, the heads the TSA time-stamps certify (each over a number of links and its root) and the heads of a witness are all checked against those roots.

This release reads nothing written by v0.3.1 or earlier, whose Merkle trees hash leaves without that flag and whose heads are the hash of the last link. The store refuses a `data_dir` they wrote and names the reason: start a new one. `esverify` refuses their bundles by version (single-record bundles are now version 4, window and one-source bundles version 5 since subject labels go by month: a version 4 bundle with a single label would read as one that names none) and points to the `esverify` of their release; window and one-source bundles of v0.4.0 (version 3) are refused the same way. Older `esverify` refuses the window and one-source bundles of this release by version, and a single-record bundle of a labelled record as a record of an unknown version. Use the `esverify` of the same release as the store. A `data_dir` of v0.4.0 is read as it is, and its records carry no labels; once a record with a label is written, v0.4.0 no longer starts on that `data_dir`.
