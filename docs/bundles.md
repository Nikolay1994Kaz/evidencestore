# Bundles over a window of time

[← README](../README.md)

A single-record bundle proves one record and says nothing about the records the auditor was not given. A bundle over a window of time (RFC 3339 times, by the store's receive time) answers "everything received between FROM and TO" and also proves that nothing is left out. It is bounded by two TSA time-stamps: the last one taken more than a second before FROM and the first one taken more than two minutes after TO. A time-stamp certifies the chain up to the moment the store asked for it; whatever entered the chain while the request was under way lies after that point and is certified only by the next time-stamp. The store does not use a TSA answer that took longer than 30 seconds, and a receive time lags real time by at most 55 seconds, so such a record carries a receive time later than two minutes before the time-stamp, outside the window. Nor does a token enter the chain before the records received before its TSA answer, and it lies in a link of its own: while the store waits for those records, new records wait a moment before they are taken in, and if one of them is still being written five seconds later (a stalled disk), the store drops the answer and the next time-stamp is taken on schedule. Receive times follow the last time-stamp and the host's monotonic clock, which stands still while the host is suspended or its VM paused: after such a pause they lag by its length until the next time-stamp. The store asks for one as soon as the host clock jumps ahead of its estimate, and a TSA answer whose time lies beyond the receive-time window is not used: the store pauses the intake until the records already received reach the chain (collectors retry on `503`; a record still being written after five seconds keeps the intake paused until it lands, and then the store moves its estimate to that TSA time without asking again), moves its estimate to the TSA time and stamps again, so whatever arrived during that request lies before the time-stamp and every receive time follows one estimate or the other. There are two kinds:

- `evidencestore proof -source S -from FROM -to TO [-dek]`: every record of one source, for an auditor from outside. Other sources stay closed.
- `evidencestore proof -window -from FROM -to TO [-source S -dek]`: every record of every source, for an investigation inside the organization.

**One source.** Every evidence leaf carries the hash of the previous leaf of its source, so the records of a source form a chain of their own inside the store's chain. Right before each TSA request the store writes the source tails, the hash of each source's latest leaf, alone in a link, and that link is what the time-stamp certifies. The lower end of the bundle is the tails of the time-stamp before the window. The upper end needs the opposite bound, that everything put into the chain after it came after the window, and a time-stamp over an end does not give it: one can be taken over old tails at any time and appended to the chain without rewriting anything. So the bundle carries the token of the time-stamp that closes the window, which lies in the chain before the upper end, and the upper end is the tails of the first time-stamp after that token: whatever follows the token entered the chain after its time. A one-source bundle is therefore ready one time-stamp later than a window bundle. It carries both ends and every record of S between them with its Merkle path; in those paths other sources' leaves are only hashes. `esverify` checks that each record refers to the one before it, from the lower tails up to the upper ones: a record left out breaks the reference of the next one or leaves the chain short of the upper tails. Each end is certified by its own time-stamp, so moving an end means rewriting the chain after it and taking fresh time-stamps. A witness (`-heads`) catches such a rewrite; without one, a rewrite made within about an hour of the window cannot be told from an honest bundle by T2 alone. `esverify` also checks every receive time against the time-stamps of the ends. In the tails each source is named by a hash of its name, and its entry, with the kind of the source, is sealed with a salt only the store can derive; the bundle opens only S. Of the other sources the auditor sees how many there are, could test a guessed name, and sees the pace of the whole store: for every link between the ends, when it was made and how many leaves it holds. Their record numbers and which of them wrote what stay closed. The tails are the store's own statements, written when it took the time-stamps: a store that understated them would have lied at that moment, and a window bundle or the hidden record's own bundle shows the lie. For gateway journal records the bundle also carries the store's verdict from intake: records found broken are named in the report, and their bodies are not opened (a single-record bundle shows what is wrong). The tails name a gateway journal as such, so its bundle does not verify without a verdict for every record, with bodies or without; a source whose kind changed between the ends (its configuration did) does not verify either.

The bundle names its source itself, and `esverify` does not know which source you meant: compare the name in the report, printed in quotes, with the one you expect. A name the tails do not list proves nothing: such a bundle verifies and holds no records, whether the source really has none yet or someone took a real bundle, changed the name to a similar one and removed its records. The report says so instead of "no records", and `-json` gives `"source_named": false`.

**Every source.** The window bundle carries every leaf of the chain between the two time-stamps. `esverify` recomputes the Merkle root of every link from its leaves and checks that the leaves continue right after the lower head: a record left out, added or moved breaks a root. It verifies every time-stamp in the stretch and checks each record's receive time against the time-stamps around its link: not later than the first one over it, not earlier than a minute before the latest time-stamp whose token lies before it, and later than two minutes before any time-stamp whose certified head it lies after. "Latest" is by the time in the time-stamp, not by place: a copy of an old token can be added anywhere later in the chain. Records after the head the upper time-stamp certifies are not in the report and cannot be attached: nothing in the bundle certifies them. Their receive times must still be later than two minutes before that time-stamp, so a store that held its request back to push records of the window behind the certified head is caught. The report lists every source in the window with its record numbers, recorded gaps, silences, a lost collector and seq conflicts, and names number holes that have no recorded gap. It shows the metadata of every source: names, record numbers, receive times, digests. Bodies come only for one source, with `-source S -dek`.

Give your own window with `esverify -from FROM -to TO`; without it the window named in the bundle is used, and the report says so. `esverify -bodies DIR` writes the bodies out byte for byte. With no record received in the window it says so and exits 0, so a script can go window by window; with records and no body to open it names the reason and exits 2; when it writes some bodies and not others — a month sent as leaves, evidence that comes without its key — it names them on stderr and exits 0. A window bundle names the source its keys are for only in an unsigned mark (`keys_for`), so it counts as empty only when no source has a record in the window, and keys for a source with no records there are named as such (code 2); a one-source bundle knows its source. The window can be closed only by a time-stamp taken more than two minutes after its end: the store takes one every `tsa.every` while records arrive and at least once an hour, and `evidencestore stamp` takes one at once. One bundle spans up to 250 000 leaves of the chain; split longer windows. The window is the store's receive time, not the time written in a line. A record the store held back and put into the chain after the time-stamp that closes the window is not in the window bundle; the collector's promise for it shows the missed deadline (`esverify -promise` on the record's own bundle). That check rests on the time-stamps alone, not on the store's word: `esverify -promise` checks that no time-stamp taken more than a minute after the receive time lies before the record in the chain, whatever deadline the promise names. A store of this release names a deadline from 55 seconds plus two chain ticks to a minute (the collector checks that it is no later than a minute), so the check is at most a few seconds looser than that deadline: a shorter one cannot be checked fairly against TSA time, since a receive time lags it by up to 55 seconds. The time-stamp that closes the window is taken more than two minutes after its end, so a record held back past it fails the check. The single-record bundle carries every leaf from the head that the time-stamp before the record (T1) certifies to the record's link, and `esverify` checks them against the link roots, so no time-stamp in between can be left out. Not from T1's own token: the store picks T1, and a copy of an old token, or a token it held back, can be put anywhere later in the chain, but not before the head it certifies. Link times play no part, so `esverify -promise` passes for an honest store however long a record takes to reach the chain: a time-stamp waits for the records received before its TSA answer, and is dropped if one of them is still being written five seconds later.

**What opens.** The time-stamps rarely fall on the window, so a bundle runs from one to the other and holds more records than you asked about. Completeness needs only their leaves. With `-dek`, only the records received in the window come with their evidence and record keys; the other records between the ends are leaves: number, receive time and digest, no body (in a gateway journal also the store's verdict, which names the session, the entry type and its hash). `esverify` refuses a bundle with evidence that leaves out a record of the window, also when your own `-from` and `-to` are wider than the bundle's window, and `-bodies` writes only the records of your window. A month whose key is destroyed or awaits destruction is the exception: there are no bodies to open, and the store sends all its records as leaves, so their headers (and the subject labels in them, see below) are not shown. `esverify` accepts a leaf only inside the bundle's own window and only in a month for which the bundle carries no evidence at all. Outside its window the store never attaches evidence, so your wider window that reaches into another month needs a bundle over your window, whatever its keys. In a month that has evidence every record of the window must come with its own, so one record cannot be taken out, unless it is the only record of the window in its month: then it goes as its whole month, and the report names the month. A month sent as leaves looks the same whether its key is gone or someone took all its evidence out of the bundle: the `with_keys` and `keys_for` marks are not signed, and the report names such months and says that this bundle cannot tell which. Ask the store for the same bundle again: while the month key lives, a fresh bundle carries the month's evidence. Once the key is destroyed, only the store's key receipt speaks for it. Every change of a month key's state is a leaf of the chain under the time-stamps; a window bundle over the time the key was closed (`evidencestore proof -window -from -to`) carries it, and `esverify` lists each receipt with its link and operation and says which ones close a month. Only `pending-destruction` (until a later cancel: a `deactivated` receipt with the operation `cancel-destroy`) and `destroyed` close a month; `deactivated` alone does not: the store deactivates every past month a few minutes after it ends, and its key lives. A bundle does not carry the time it was issued, so a receipt does not show that the month was closed already when the bundle was issued. A receipt enters the chain once the change has reached the key backup, and it is the store's own statement. A window bundle that sends a month as leaves also says what the receipts of its own stretch show about that month. Evidence that comes without its record key in such a bundle was stripped of it, or the store held the key back: a month whose key is closed comes as leaves, not as evidence without keys. A month key closed while a bundle is being issued does not leak the month's headers: at the end the store checks again every month it sent as evidence and builds the bundle anew, with that month as leaves (the admin API answers `409` if the keys keep changing). `proof -window -source S -dek` keeps the same rules for the records of S.

**One subject.** A source is one system, and one system can write about many people: an AI agent serves thousands of clients through one log, and they all are one source. Set `subject_field` on such a source (see [Configuration](configuration.md)), and at intake the store labels each JSON line with a pseudonym of that field's value and writes the field's name next to it. `evidencestore proof -source S -subject c-104233 -from FROM -to TO` then opens the records of that subject in the window and no others. The other records of the window, in months the bundle names a label for, come as encrypted evidence without keys: their labels can be read, their bodies cannot; a month it names no label for comes as leaves (see erasure below). Records of the source outside the window are leaves, as above.

It is the same chain of the source, so no record of the source is left out; which of them are the subject's rests on the labels the store set at intake. `esverify` also requires the evidence of every record of the window in a month the bundle names a label for (records of a month it names no label for come as leaves, see erasure below, and evidence in such a month is refused: the store sends that month as leaves, so a label was taken out of the bundle), checks that the records under the label were labelled by the field the bundle names and that every opened body names the subject in that field, and refuses a record under another label that comes with a key. The report, its check line and `-json` split the window's records the same way: the subject's records (under its label in months where one of its bodies opens) and how many of them are open, records under its label in months where none opens, and the other records, closed; `-bodies` writes only the open ones. When no record of the window is under the label — also when the records lie only in months the bundle names no label for — `-bodies` says that and exits 0, as it does for a window with no records, so a script can go window by window; it does not show that the subject has no records (see below). When the label has records in the window and none of them opens, it names the reason and exits 2; when some open, it writes those, names every month of the window where none opens, and the report counts the rest.

What a subject bundle does not show:

- The store sets the label, when it receives a record: it cannot change, since it lies under the digest in the chain, but a store dishonest at intake can file a line under another label or none. The chain stays whole, the collector's promise stays valid and the bundle verifies, unlike a line that is simply not delivered, which leaves a gap in the numbers.
- The value is compared as written: `C-1` and `c-1`, or `1` and `1.0`, are two subjects (`104233` and `"104233"` are one). A string is compared after its escapes are decoded (`"c\u002d1"` is `c-1`), a number as it is spelled. A line that names someone else is not under this label. A string that is not valid UTF-8 or holds half of a surrogate pair (`"\ud800"`) gets no label: a JSON parser turns both into U+FFFD, and different values would become one subject. `-subject` with such a value, or with one longer than 256 bytes, is refused: no record is under it.
- Lines that name no subject carry no label: the report counts those of the window, and `evidencestore_records_without_subject_total` counts them at intake. That is a line without a string or number in that field, with an empty value, one longer than 256 bytes or one that is not valid UTF-8, with the field twice, or that is not a whole JSON object. Records received before the source had `subject_field` carry no label either, whatever their lines say: a subject bundle never opens them.
- The label is an HMAC under a label key of the record's month, derived from that month key, because a client number is short enough to try them all, so an auditor cannot compute a label; the store can. An open body of a month ties that month's label to the subject, and only that month's: a body opened in September says nothing about the October label, and records under a label of a month with no open body are not called the subject's. With no body of the subject open in the window, nothing ties any label, and the report says so: ask for a window that opens one of its records in those months, or compare the labels with a bundle that does; a bundle that names no label at all has nothing to compare, and no window opens a month whose key is closed. A wider window is not always possible (see the limit below).
- A month of the window where no body of the subject opens shows nothing about the subject's records there. The bundle's labels are not signed, so it can name any label for such a month — a random one, the subject's label of another month, someone else's — and the subject's records under the real label then count among the others. An honest bundle where the subject has no records in that month looks the same, so the report, its `Subject:` line and `-bodies` name every such month, whatever the bundle names for it.
- Of the other records of the window in months it names a label for, the bundle shows the number, receive time, length and label. A client keeps one label within a month of a source, so the records of one pseudonymous client can be linked within that month, in one bundle and between bundles, but not across months: each month has labels of its own, and a bundle across a month boundary names a label for each month.
- A record under the label that comes without a key has no body to show: the key was removed from this bundle, or the store held it back. (A month whose key is destroyed or awaits destruction gets no label at all, see below.) The report counts them, and `-bodies` names them when it writes the others.
- A window takes the same limit of 250 000 leaves, and the evidence of every record of the window travels whole: for all of a client's history, ask window by window.
- Erasure stays per source and month, and it takes the labels with it as far as they can be computed. The label key of a month comes from its month key (HKDF), so once that key is destroyed no one can compute a label of that month any more, the store included. While the key awaits destruction the store does not compute labels either, but the key is still in `keyring.dat` and its backup: a cancelled destruction brings back the same labels and the bodies. A subject bundle names no label for such a month and sends its records as leaves only (number, receive time, digest), without their labels or lengths; the report counts them as records whose owner cannot be told.
- Erasure makes labels uncomputable, and the store stops showing them. The labels stay in the records under their digests, so a label already handed out in an earlier subject bundle stays that client's pseudonym in that month, and would match the client's records wherever their headers are shown. No bundle shows them by default: subject, one-source and window bundles send such a month as leaves, and `proof -source S -seq N` refuses a labelled record of such a month and says why. When the header is needed — a dispute over the collector's promise, which a single-record bundle settles — `proof … -show-label` issues it, and then whoever holds the label sees that this record is the client's.
- That a month key is gone is the store's word: the claim is not signed, so anyone can take a month's label out of a bundle together with all its evidence (the label alone, with the evidence left, is refused), and the report says only that the bundle names no label for those months. How to tell such a month from a stripped one — a fresh bundle while the key lives, the store's key receipt once it is destroyed — is in "What opens".

A single-record bundle carries the record's evidence, header included. For a record with a subject label whose month key is destroyed or awaits destruction the store asks for `-show-label` first (see above); a record without a label is issued as before, its header names no one. A single-record bundle carries every leaf from the head that T1 certifies to the record's link, so that `esverify -promise` sees every time-stamp before the record and the store cannot hide one. Only the time-stamps lie there whole; every other leaf is just its hash, so the bundle names no other source and no other record. What shows is the pace of the whole store, as in any bundle: how many leaves each link holds and when the link was made. In the Merkle tree of a link each leaf enters together with a flag that says whether it is a time-stamp, and a leaf shown by its hash counts as not one: a time-stamp shown that way breaks its link's root, and a time-stamp written into the chain with the flag unset breaks the root wherever it is shown whole, at the edges of a window or as T1, so it closes no window. A shown hash costs about 70 bytes as the store serves the bundle, and the bundle still grows with the records taken in since T1: with one record a second and a time-stamp every 10 minutes the whole bundle is up to about 165 KB, with 100 records a second about 4 MB, and while the TSA is unreachable it grows by everything taken in since the last time-stamp.

Every bundle ends in a head signed when the bundle is served: a checkpoint, the number of links and the root of a Merkle tree (RFC 6962) over all of them. The bundle carries the links it needs and, before the first of them, the frontier of that tree, at most one hash per bit of the link number, so `esverify` computes the root at every link of the bundle: the signed head, the heads the TSA time-stamps certify (each over a number of links and its root) and the heads of a witness are all checked against those roots.

This release reads nothing written by v0.3.1 or earlier, whose Merkle trees hash leaves without that flag and whose heads are the hash of the last link. The store refuses a `data_dir` they wrote and names the reason: start a new one. `esverify` refuses their bundles by version (single-record bundles are now version 4, window and one-source bundles version 5 since subject labels go by month: a version 4 bundle with a single label would read as one that names none) and points to the `esverify` of their release; window and one-source bundles of v0.4.0 (version 3) are refused the same way. Older `esverify` refuses the window and one-source bundles of this release by version, and a single-record bundle of a labelled record as a record of an unknown version. Use the `esverify` of the same release as the store. Window and one-source bundles of version 4 came only from the development build of 7 November 2026 and no released `esverify` reads them; `esverify` says so. That build also wrote records with labels from `sign.key` (record version 2): the store refuses to start on such a `data_dir` and names the reason, since their labels would match no one under month keys. A `data_dir` of v0.4.0 is read as it is, and its records carry no labels; once a record with a label is written, v0.4.0 no longer starts on that `data_dir`.

## Revoked TSA certificates

A TSA certificate can be revoked after it signed time-stamps: the TSA closed or changed its key, or its key was stolen. `esverify` is offline and knows only what the auditor brings: the CRLs of the CAs that issued the TSA certificates (`-tsa-crl FILE`, DER or PEM, one CRL or several in a file — text in UTF-8 around PEM blocks, such as `openssl crl -text` writes, is fine, but a file with a damaged block boundary, or binary data beside the blocks, is refused, so that no CRL in it is lost unseen — the flag repeated or the files separated by commas, an empty name being an error) and own lists (`-tsa-revoked FILE`, repeated the same way: `[{"cert_sha256": HEX, "revoked": RFC3339, "reason": "keyCompromise", "invalidity": RFC3339}]`), for a TSA that publishes no CRL or a compromise learned elsewhere; a list is read strictly, one JSON list per file and each field of an entry once. Pass every CRL of a CA you have, old ones too: an entry in any CRL signed with the key the CA issued the certificate with counts, whatever its scope or age, since a CA may drop a certificate from its CRLs once it expires and an entry can only add suspicion; only a certificate on hold is released, by a later CRL that can tell its status and no longer lists it at all (one that lists it with another reason made the hold permanent, and the date of the hold stands). The status of a certificate now (that it is not revoked) is told by the latest CRL that can tell it: verified with a certificate of the CA, in a chain of the bundle, that may sign CRLs (a CA with `cRLSign`), covering the certificate (its issuing distribution point, if it has one, includes it), issued before the certificate expired or keeping expired ones (`expiredCertsOnCRL`), and not more than an hour after the check by its clock: fetch a fresh CRL just before the check, and keep those of the past for certificates that have expired. With no such CRL the report says that the certificate was not checked, and why; one past its next update is used, and the report says that a revocation after it is not seen. A CRL is verified with the key of the CA in the chain of the time-stamp. One that names the CA but is signed with another key tells no status; when it lists the certificate, `esverify` looks for a certificate with the CA's name and the key that signed it among the certificates of the chains of the bundle and of your `-tsa-trust` — not among those a token merely carries, which nobody signed. When the signatures from that certificate up lead to the root of the certificate's chain, the CA changed its key and revoked the certificate with the new one (RFC 5280, 6.3.3 f): its entries count against the certificate as those of its own CRL would, and a hold in it is released by a later CRL of the same key that no longer lists the certificate. A root here is its name and key (RFC 5280, 6.1.1 d), and the dates of the certificates on the way are not checked: a root or a CA renewed on the same key is the same, and such a CRL can only add suspicion. Otherwise the report says that the CRL lists the certificate and is not used, and when that CRL is newer than the one that tells the status, the certificate counts as not checked: give the certificate of the CA's new key in `-tsa-trust`, with the CA certificates between it and the root. A CRL of another CA of the same name gets the same answer: an issuer's name and a serial number name one certificate (RFC 5280, 4.1.2.2), and `esverify` cannot tell another CA from this one under a key it has no certificate of. A CRL with an extension given twice, and one of attribute certificates only, are refused too. A delta CRL, an indirect one and one with a critical extension `esverify` cannot process are refused (exit code 2): pass the full CRL the CA signed itself. When that CRL points to delta CRLs, the report says that a revocation published only in a delta since then is not seen. Without either flag the report says that the revocation was not checked.

The rule is that of RFC 3161, section 4. A certificate revoked without compromise (`unspecified`, `affiliationChanged`, `superseded`, `cessationOfOperation`) keeps the time-stamps made before the revocation, and the one made at or after it fails its check. Any other reason (`keyCompromise`, `cACompromise`, `certificateHold`, `privilegeWithdrawn`, `aACompromise`), or no reason in the CRL entry, makes the time of its time-stamps prove nothing: whoever holds the key can put any time in. Such a time-stamp counts only if the bundle shows it existed before the cutoff (the revocation, or the entry's `invalidityDate` if earlier; ETSI EN 319 102-1 calls this a proof of existence): a time-stamp of another TSA, or a cosignature of a witness given with `-witness-key`, over a head that holds its token and dated before the cutoff. A proof dated before the time-stamp it vouches for (its clock is behind) counts from the time of that time-stamp, since the head that holds a token cannot be certified before the token was made: a time-stamp dated after the cutoff is rescued by nothing. Every certificate of the chain but the root is checked against its issuer's CRL, the root only against the own list, and a certificate found in several chains (its CA certified by two roots) is judged by all of them together: its CA's CRL counts when one of the CA's certificates may sign CRLs, and its entries count even when none may, since a time-stamp may carry only such a one. Such a reason makes the key suspect, not only the certificate, from the earliest cutoff of that key in the bundle: every time-stamp of the bundle — the store's own and those of the second TSA alike — each chain of which holds a certificate of a key under suspicion (any certificate of it: issued again, or by another CA) needs a proof of its own, and none of them counts as a proof. The certificates you give in `-tsa-trust` and those the tokens carry count for the key too, though no chain goes through them, and they are judged the same way — by the CRLs of the CA that issued them, a released hold released, a CRL under a new key of the CA counted, and one that lists such a certificate, under a key `esverify` cannot tie to the root, with an entry that would put the key under suspicion (and newer than the CRL that tells that certificate's status, or of any age when none does) makes the certificates of the chains with that key not checked; one of yours whose CA is not known (its certificate is not given) is judged by every CRL of that CA's name, while one a token carries is then left alone: a key renewed on the same key whose current certificate is revoked is under suspicion also in a time-stamp that chains through its earlier certificate, expired and so dropped from the CRLs. Give every certificate of a key you know of in `-tsa-trust`; a revoked one that `esverify` never sees, it cannot connect to the key. The rule of no reason is for the TSA's certificate — any certificate with the key of a TSA, whatever its basic constraints say: a CA certificate revoked with no reason (by a CRL or by your list) puts the chains through it under suspicion, not its key — a cross-certificate whose CA stopped cross-certifying leaves the other chain in force, and a sole chain needs a proof, as for a TSA; say `cACompromise` or `keyCompromise` to put the key itself under suspicion. A time-stamp counts as the proof of another only when the revocation of its own certificates was checked (ETSI EN 319 102-1): give the CRLs of the second TSA's CA too, or the report says that its time-stamp would show it, but its own status is not known. Once the second TSA's certificate has expired, its CA may drop it from its CRLs, and only a CRL of that CA from before the expiry tells its status: keep the CRLs of the second TSA's CA while its certificate is valid, or rely on a cosigning witness, whose proof needs no CRL.

The store makes such proof available in two ways. A second TSA of another operator (`tsa.archive`, [Configuration](configuration.md)): every `tsa.every` it time-stamps the head the copy holds on its own, keeps the token in `data_dir/tsa/archive.jsonl` (it goes to the copy, comes back with `restore`, and stays in use when `tsa.archive` changes or is removed), and every bundle carries, for each time-stamp in it, the first such token of each key of the second TSAs the journal has seen over a head at or after its link — with a proof of a few hashes when that head lies past the end of the bundle. A second TSA is replaced when it is no longer trusted, and then the token of the next one is the proof. A cosigning witness (`witness.cosigners`, [Copy and witness](copy-and-witness.md)): the store gives it the head of the link of every token, and a single-record bundle carries, besides the cosignature that fixes the record, the one over the link of the T2 token. With either, a single-record bundle carries the leaf of the T2 token. Those tokens bound no record and do not move T1, T2 or the receive times, which follow the clock of the store's own TSA: they only vouch for its time-stamps once it is compromised. Give `esverify` the root of the second TSA in `-tsa-trust` too, or the witness's key with `-witness-key`; a bundle carries the certificate of a second TSA that does not put it in its tokens (the journal keeps it), and a bundle of an earlier release does not, so give that certificate in `-tsa-trust` for those. A time-stamp made shortly before the cutoff, with no later time-stamp of the second TSA or cosignature before it, still proves nothing, and a bundle that rests on it fails; when the report names a cosigned head past the end of the bundle, ask for one up to it (`evidencestore proof … -to-head N`).
