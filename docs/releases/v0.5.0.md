evidencestore is an independent witness for logs. A collector next to the log sends every line to a store on another machine, and the store can later prove any of those lines to an auditor, offline and without being trusted: the exact bytes, their place in a hash chain, a signed chain head and RFC 3161 time-stamps from before and after the record. Retention works by destroying a month's key: the bodies become unreadable in every copy, while the chain and the proofs stay valid.

This release adds a proof for one person inside a shared log, witnesses that answer for themselves, and checks of the certificates the proofs rest on. It reads a `data_dir` of v0.4.0 as it is. It also fixes defects that are in v0.4.0, one of them in `esverify`: see Fixed, and verify with this release's `esverify`.

## New in this release

- **A bundle for one subject inside a source.** One system often writes about many people: an AI agent serves thousands of clients through one log. A source can now name the field that says whose a line is (`"subject_field": "client"`); the store labels each record at intake, and `evidencestore proof -source S -subject V -from FROM -to TO` opens that subject's records in the window and no others, with the same proof that no record of the source is left out. The labels come from a key that each month key derives, so destroying a month's key destroys its labels too. Which records are the subject's rests on the label the store set at intake ([Bundles, One subject](https://github.com/Nikolay1994Kaz/evidencestore/blob/v0.5.0/docs/bundles.md)).
- **Witnesses that cosign the chain heads.** A new program, `eswitness`, run by someone other than the store's administrator, cosigns a head only if it extends the last head it cosigned for that store, and its signature carries the time. The store speaks the C2SP protocols for this, [tlog-witness](https://c2sp.org/tlog-witness) and [tlog-cosignature](https://c2sp.org/tlog-cosignature) (`witness.cosigners`, up to 8 witnesses), keeps the cosignatures and puts them into the bundles, and an auditor passes each witness's key with `esverify -witness-key`. A chain rewritten after a witness cosigned contradicts it ([Cosigning witnesses](https://github.com/Nikolay1994Kaz/evidencestore/blob/v0.5.0/docs/copy-and-witness.md#cosigning-witnesses-c2sp)).
- **`esverify -heads` goes by when the witness received a head.** Of the heads at or after the record's link it takes, for each witness, the one that witness received first, by the S3 versions list or a collector's head journal. A head signed later and added to a witness bucket under an unused number no longer outranks the honest one: this closes the known issue of v0.2.0 to v0.4.0 ([Witness of the chain heads](https://github.com/Nikolay1994Kaz/evidencestore/blob/v0.5.0/docs/copy-and-witness.md#witness-of-the-chain-heads)).
- **Revoked TSA certificates, and a second TSA.** `esverify -tsa-crl` and `-tsa-revoked` check every certificate in the chain of a time-stamp, by the rule of RFC 3161: a certificate revoked without compromise keeps the time-stamps made before the revocation, and after a compromise a time-stamp counts only if the bundle shows that it existed earlier. That proof comes from a second TSA of another operator (`tsa.archive`), which time-stamps the chain head on its own, or from a cosigning witness. Without either flag the report says that revocation was not checked ([Revoked TSA certificates](https://github.com/Nikolay1994Kaz/evidencestore/blob/v0.5.0/docs/bundles.md#revoked-tsa-certificates)).
- **A seal of your organization on the store key.** An auditor normally takes the store key from the store's operator. The organization can now vouch for it with its own X.509 certificate (`evidencestore seal-statement`, then `"seal": {"file": "seal.p7s"}`), and the auditor needs only the CA's root and the organization's name: `esverify -seal-trust CA -seal-subject ATTR=VALUE` ([A seal on the store key](https://github.com/Nikolay1994Kaz/evidencestore/blob/v0.5.0/docs/keys.md#a-seal-on-the-store-key)).
- **A security policy.** [`SECURITY.md`](https://github.com/Nikolay1994Kaz/evidencestore/blob/v0.5.0/SECURITY.md) says how to report a vulnerability and what counts as one. The parsers of outside input are now fuzzed.

## Fixed

These defects are in v0.4.0.

- **A one-source bundle with its name changed verified as "no records".** In an honest one-source bundle the source name could be changed to a look-alike (`"agent "` for `"agent"`) and the records and tail disclosures removed: `esverify` reported VERIFIED, "no records", exit code 0, and no secret was needed. A line break in the name could also paint lines into the report. Now the report prints the name in quotes, escapes it, and says that the source tails do not name it. The verdict stays VERIFIED, because an honest source without records looks the same, and `esverify` cannot be told which source you meant: compare the name in the report with the one you expect.
- **A bundle with bodies opened more than the window.** With `-dek`, a one-source bundle carried the record keys of every record of the source between the two time-stamps around the window, not only of the window asked for. Now only the records received in the window come with their keys, and `esverify` refuses a bundle with evidence that leaves a record of the window out.
- **A record changed on disk together with its checksum went unnoticed by the store itself.** `esverify` caught it, but only when someone checked the bundle of that very record. Now every start checks each record in the chain against the digest in its leaf, and the store refuses to start on one that differs: restore `data_dir` from a copy taken before the change.
- **A one-source bundle over a burst did not finish.** Every Merkle path rebuilt the tree of its whole link, so a bundle over links of thousands of leaves ran past the two-minute timeout of the admin client. With one tree per link the same bundle, byte for byte, takes 1.2 seconds instead of 360.

## Compatibility

- A `data_dir` of v0.4.0 is read as it is, and its records carry no labels. Once a record with a subject label is written (a source with `subject_field`), v0.4.0 no longer starts on that `data_dir`.
- Window and one-source bundles are now version 5; single-record bundles stay version 4. This release's `esverify` refuses the window and one-source bundles of v0.4.0 (version 3) and says why: keep v0.4.0's `esverify` for them. An older `esverify` refuses this release's window and one-source bundles, and a single-record bundle of a labelled record. Use the `esverify` of the same release as the store.
- `esverify -bodies`: a window that holds nothing of what you asked for (no records, or none under the subject's label) is now an answer, with a message and exit code 0. Exit code 2 is left for records that are there but cannot be opened. v0.4.0 documented 2 for any window without bodies.
- The check at start makes a start about twice as long and takes more memory: on a loaded test machine a month of 800,000 records took 6 seconds instead of 3, and 120 MB instead of 53.
- Collectors need no change: the intake protocol and `escollect` are as in v0.4.0.
- New and optional: `witness.cosigners`, `tsa.archive`, `seal`, and `subject_field` of a source. A fourth binary, `eswitness`, comes with an example config and a systemd unit.
- No new dependencies.

## Known issues

- **The seal checks RSA and ECDSA with SHA-2 only.** ГОСТ is refused, which leaves out the certificates the National Certification Authority of Kazakhstan issues to legal entities (ГОСТ 34.10-2015 only, since 30 April 2024).
- **Cosignatures are Ed25519 only**, and the store was tested against `eswitness`, the test vectors of the specifications and Go's reference implementation of signed notes, not yet against witnesses of other projects.
- **A bundle names its own source**: see the first item under Fixed.

All of them, with their causes: [Known limitations](https://github.com/Nikolay1994Kaz/evidencestore/blob/v0.5.0/docs/limitations.md).

## Install

Binaries: `evidencestore`, `escollect` and `eswitness` for Linux amd64 and arm64, `esverify` also for macOS (amd64, arm64) and Windows (amd64). Check them before use:

```sh
sha256sum -c SHA256SUMS --ignore-missing
gh attestation verify esverify-linux-amd64 --repo Nikolay1994Kaz/evidencestore
```

With Go 1.25.4 or newer: `go install github.com/Nikolay1994Kaz/evidencestore/cmd/esverify@v0.5.0`, likewise `cmd/evidencestore`, `cmd/escollect` and `cmd/eswitness`.

Setting up a store, collectors and a witness, with systemd unit files: [README, Install](https://github.com/Nikolay1994Kaz/evidencestore#install).

## How it was tested

- The test gate with the race detector, in CI on Go 1.25.4.
- Mutation testing. The set of injected bugs grew from 650 to 1,157. The ones written for the new code were run as that code was written, and every one makes a test fail: the subject labels (74), the witness check by receipt time (62), the cosigning witness (128), TSA revocation and the second TSA (119), the seal (37) and the token check (14). The whole set has not been run together on this release's code: the sets of the store (605) and of the cosigning witness were not run again as a whole after the last changes.
- Revocation against OpenSSL: twenty cases go both to `openssl verify -crl_check_all` and to `esverify`. They agree in seventeen, and the three differences are deliberate and named in the test. The real CRLs of the National Certification Authority of Kazakhstan (1,843 entries) parse, verify and give the same answer as OpenSSL for the real time-stamp in the test data.
- The cosigning witness end to end, with the real binaries on one machine: a bundle verifies with the witness's key and fails without the cosignature or with another key of the same name, and a witness restarted or a store killed with `kill -9` goes on cosigning.
- A model of an AI agent's log (7 November, synthetic data, one machine): a month of traffic, 794,565 records, went in at 2,687 records a second; `kill -9` of the store and of the collector lost and repeated nothing; a bundle for one client out of 18,935 records of 799 clients in the window opened that client's 75 records and no others.
- Simulations on this release's code, short runs, no failures: the store with crashes, 120 scenarios of 200 steps; a dishonest store owner, 300 scenarios; the receive-time clock, 200 scenarios; the collector, 150 scenarios in each of four worlds. The long runs made for v0.4.0, thousands of scenarios each, have not been repeated on this code. Both demos pass.
- The new code went through review rounds by AI agents, each followed by passes over the fixes; the findings and their fixes (P-46 to P-230) are in [`docs/dev/FINDINGS.md`](https://github.com/Nikolay1994Kaz/evidencestore/blob/v0.5.0/docs/dev/FINDINGS.md), in Russian.

Details: [docs/testing.md](https://github.com/Nikolay1994Kaz/evidencestore/blob/v0.5.0/docs/testing.md).

## Before you use it

- Until 1.0 the on-disk formats (evidence log, chain, keyring) may change; each release will describe the way over.
- The pilot cryptography is not certified: AES-256-GCM from the Go standard library, root keys in files. The root store is a plug-in point for an HSM.
- Read [Known limitations](https://github.com/Nikolay1994Kaz/evidencestore#known-limitations).
- There is no container image yet.
