evidencestore is an independent witness for logs. A collector next to the log sends every line to a store on another machine, and the store can later prove any of those lines to an auditor, offline and without being trusted: the exact bytes, their place in a hash chain, a signed chain head and RFC 3161 time-stamps from before and after the record. Retention works by destroying a month's key: the bodies become unreadable in every copy, while the chain and the proofs stay valid.

## New in this release

- **Bundles over a window of time.** A single-record bundle proves one record and says nothing about the records the auditor was not given. `evidencestore proof -window -from FROM -to TO` gives every record the store put into its chain between two TSA time-stamps around the window, and `esverify` checks that none was left out, added or moved; the report lists every source with its record numbers, recorded gaps, silences and lost collectors. `evidencestore proof -source S -from FROM -to TO` gives the same answer for one source, for an auditor from outside: of the other sources it shows only how many there are and the pace of the whole store. Every record now refers to the one before it from its source, and the source tails the store writes right before each time-stamp fix both ends.
- **The promise is checked against time-stamps, not against the store's word.** The collector holds a signed promise for every record it delivered. `esverify -promise` now checks it against the TSA time-stamps alone: no time-stamp taken more than a minute after the record's receive time may lie before the record in the chain. A record the store held back past a window fails this check, and an honest store passes it whatever its clocks did: a time-stamp token enters the chain only after the records received before the TSA answered. For that, a single-record bundle carries every leaf from the head that the earlier time-stamp certifies to the record's link.
- **The collector checks the store's promises**: a receive time more than a minute later than the store's answer by the collector's own clock, or a deadline more than a minute after the receive time, is logged as an error, and the promise is kept as evidence against the store (`promises-ahead.jsonl`, `promises-deadline.jsonl`).
- **Collector heartbeat.** With nothing to send, the collector reports every `heartbeat_every` (30 seconds by default) with the last number it issued. A collector that stays quiet longer than the source's `expect_contact` (2 minutes by default) shows as lost, in the chain and in `/metrics` (`evidencestore_source_contact_lost`), and as back when it returns.
- **A failed write of the evidence log stops time-stamps until the store restarts**, and the restart writes the records not yet in the chain to disk again before they enter it.
- **Safer start:** the key backup, the roots mirror and a `file://` copy or witness directory are no longer created by `init` or the store (a missing one looks like an unmounted disk; `init` refuses to run without the first two), and a new `sign.key` is made only for an empty chain and an empty key backup.

## Compatibility

- Single-record bundles are now version 3. They grow with the records taken in since the earlier time-stamp, about 210 bytes per leaf: up to about 250 KB with one record a second and a time-stamp every 10 minutes. `esverify` v0.2.0 does not read them. This `esverify` still verifies bundles of versions 1 and 2, but `-promise` refuses them: ask the store for the record's bundle again, it builds version 3 for any record, older ones included.
- `chain.promise_mmd` must be at least 55 seconds plus two chain ticks and at most a minute (the demo scripts of earlier releases had 10 seconds), and `chain.tick` at most 2.5 seconds; a configuration outside these limits is refused at start.
- Upgrade the store first and the collectors soon after: collectors of v0.2.0 send no heartbeat, so with this store their quiet minutes read as a lost collector, and collectors of this release flag every promise whose deadline is more than a minute after its receive time, which a v0.2.0 store with `chain.promise_mmd` above a minute gives.
- Keep the collectors' clocks within a minute of real time (NTP): a collector whose clock runs more than a minute behind logs an error for every record and fills `promises-ahead.jsonl`.
- Data written by v0.2.0 stays readable: upgrade by replacing the binaries. There is no way back: once this release has put a record into the chain, v0.2.0 does not start on that `data_dir`, nor on one restored from a copy this release wrote. Records from v0.2.0 carry no reference to the previous record, so for a window of them the store refuses a one-source bundle and points to `-window`.
- Records from v0.2.0 are held to a minute after their receive time, not to the deadline in their promise. v0.2.0 did not hold a time-stamp back for the records in flight, so its record that took more than about five seconds to reach the chain across a TSA answer, on a host whose clock ran close to 55 seconds behind the TSA, can fail `-promise` although the store kept its promise. This `esverify` also checks the time of the link that holds each time-stamp token it is shown: a chain written by v0.2.0 on a host whose clock ran more than a minute behind the TSA when it took a time-stamp fails that check in the bundles that show that token.

## Install

Binaries: `evidencestore` and `escollect` for Linux amd64 and arm64, `esverify` also for macOS (amd64, arm64) and Windows (amd64). Check them before use:

```sh
sha256sum -c SHA256SUMS --ignore-missing
gh attestation verify esverify-linux-amd64 --repo Nikolay1994Kaz/evidencestore
```

With Go 1.25.4 or newer: `go install github.com/Nikolay1994Kaz/evidencestore/cmd/esverify@v0.3.0`, likewise `cmd/evidencestore` and `cmd/escollect`.

Setting up a store and collectors, with systemd unit files: [README, Install](https://github.com/Nikolay1994Kaz/evidencestore#install).

## How it was tested

- The test gate with the race detector, in CI on Go 1.25.4.
- Mutation testing: 578 injected bugs on this release's code, 28–29 October, each caught by a test. Six survived the first run, because tests saw what they broke only through the old promise check; tests added the same night catch them (Q-84 in [`docs/FINDINGS.md`](https://github.com/Nikolay1994Kaz/evidencestore/blob/v0.3.0/docs/FINDINGS.md)).
- Simulations on this release's code, no failures: a dishonest store owner, 2,000 scenarios (each of the 632 window bundles that left a record out was exposed by that record's promise); the receive-time clock, 1,000 scenarios; the store with crashes, 2,000 scenarios of 300 steps (37,307 crashes, 6,727 restores from the copy, 1,395 disks that lied about fsync); the collector, 2,000 scenarios in each of four worlds and 150 per world against the real store.
- Live runs on the release candidate, with test hooks: an upgrade from v0.2.0 with `kill -9` during intake (all 632 promises verify), and an evidence log whose fsync fails (no time-stamp until restart, then all 244 promises verify). The demos pass.
- The code of this release went through review rounds by AI agents (two independent reviewers, a tester and a judge) until a round found nothing critical; the findings and their fixes are in [`docs/FINDINGS.md`](https://github.com/Nikolay1994Kaz/evidencestore/blob/v0.3.0/docs/FINDINGS.md).

Details: [README, How it was tested](https://github.com/Nikolay1994Kaz/evidencestore#how-it-was-tested).

## Before you use it

- Until 1.0 the on-disk formats (evidence log, chain, keyring) may change; each release will describe the way over.
- The pilot cryptography is not certified: AES-256-GCM from the Go standard library, root keys in files. The root store is a plug-in point for an HSM.
- Read [Known limitations](https://github.com/Nikolay1994Kaz/evidencestore#known-limitations).
- There is no container image yet.
